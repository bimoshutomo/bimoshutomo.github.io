The situation

Trans7 got the first Indonesian TV premiere of Parasite. That sounds like a win, but local TV had a reputation for cheap, low-effort programming. Announced normally, it gets one reaction: “Parasite? On local TV?”

The idea

We used the mismatch. Parasite's poster was rebuilt as a sinetron poster, with the melodramatic fonts, floating heads and taglines every Indonesian grew up reading. We posted it with no explanation.

The execution

I directed the creative team on the parody. It had to be funny to people who know sinetron posters by heart, so the details had to be right. With no context, people worked it out themselves: confusion first, then recognition, then shares.

The result

Other people's accounts carried it, not ours. The top quote tweet got 27.1K likes and 7,000+ retweets, and Ernest Prakasa shared it. K-culture pages and meme accounts kept it going.

In one week, 28 tweets reached 1M+ impressions and 209K engagements on Twitter, a 7.29% engagement rate. Tempo and Tribun News covered it.

The situation

Part of Indonesia Kaya's job is keeping Indonesian culture alive online. Culture content usually reads like a museum caption, so people scroll past. The better stories are already around us: a market, a monument, a street corner nobody looks at twice.

The idea

Dig the culture up. Pick a place people already know and pull out the history underneath. Built as a one-off, with a structure that could repeat.

The execution

I made an episode template: a place people recognize, a hook that changes how they see it, and a payoff that is real history. Episode one was Jejak Blok Kebayoran. Later episodes covered markets named after days of the week, Portuguese traditions still alive in Kampung Tugu, and Indonesian heroes' names on Dutch street signs. That last one ran as a reel and as a full article on indonesiakaya.com.

The result

Episode one did 830K+ views and 22K+ engagements on Instagram. That was enough to turn a one-off into a permanent series.

Indonesia Menari

11 cities dancing at once. One IG Stories sequence, live within 30 minutes.

Client
Indonesia Kaya
Year
2025
Role
Campaign strategy and workflow design
Top result

The workflow

  1. 01Capture

    11 cities shooting to one standard, at the same second

  2. 02Collect

    Every city hands off through the same protocol

  3. 03Sequence

    11 frames assembled in a locked order

  4. 04Publish

    Live to IG Stories in under 30 minutes

The situation

Indonesia Menari has thousands of people dancing in 11 cities at the exact same second. It happens live, once, and can't be reshot. Turning that into one IG Stories sequence is a logistics problem before it is a creative one.

The idea

Design the workflow along with the content. One 11-part IG Stories sequence, one city per frame, published in a single run within 30 minutes of the event ending.

The execution

I built the pipeline end to end. Every city shot to the same capture standard so the frames would cut together. Footage went through one collection and handoff protocol, the order was locked before the day, and publishing was timed to land inside the 30-minute window on the first attempt.

The result

11 cities in one IG Stories sequence, published in under 30 minutes from a single live take.

The situation

A new stage musical with no online audience and a launch date getting closer. A poster can't make anyone feel what it's like to sit in that theatre.

The approach

Sell the feeling, not the ticket. I built the socials around ground-level POV storytelling that puts the viewer inside the show: the rehearsal, backstage, the second before the curtain goes up. Once people feel it, the ticket link makes sense.

The result

The situation

Indonesia Kaya wanted travel and food recommendations that feel real, not another “top 10 things to do” list that reads like a tourist board wrote it. The best guide to a place is someone who lives there.

The insight

The best source is the locals themselves. AKAMSI, short for Anak Kampung Se-Indonesia, asked people to brag about their hometown and name the food and places they'd send a friend to.

The direction

I then turned the raw comments into something usable. The best submissions were curated by region and pinned into a Google Maps playlist, embedded in the article on IndonesiaKaya.com. You read the story, tap a pin and navigate there.

The result

200+ curated spots across Indonesia, all from what locals sent in.

The situation

COVID shut down normal production, and terrestrial TV needed to be where audiences were: online, live and unscripted. There was no social live streaming playbook to copy.

The idea

Build original programs made for social, and make them sponsor-ready from episode one.

The execution

Masak Asik, a low-budget cook-along, and Ask the Doctor, a sponsored health Q&A, went live on three platforms at once, picked per program from TikTok, YouTube, Facebook and Instagram. Brand integration was part of the format from the start.

The result

This became the starting point of Trans7's social live streaming: a repeatable live model that ran outside terrestrial TV.
